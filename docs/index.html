Learning to Correct Geometry in Generated Videos
Authors: Weijie Lyu, Tiancheng Shen, Jason Li, Yujing Wang, Xiaoyuan Wang, Haobo Yuan, Yizhou Zhao, Ming-Hsuan Yang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-16T17:59:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:59:55+00:00
Comments: Accepted by NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://weijielyu.github.io/VGC
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Generated videos often lose geometric consistency under camera motion: structures warp under viewpoint change, parallax becomes inconsistent, and smooth camera movement may collapse into shot-like transitions. We argue that this failure is largely a data problem. In web-scale video corpora, most visible motion comes from dynamic objects captured by static or weakly moving cameras, leaving only sparse supervision for viewpoint-induced appearance changes. To address this, we propose Video Geometry Corrector (VGC), a post-hoc video-to-video model trained on synthetic paired supervision. Starting from real videos with diverse camera motion and pose annotations, we construct VG-Pairs with a synthetic paired-supervision data generation pipeline. Each video clip is treated as a geometry-consistent target and paired with a distorted counterpart synthesized by a pretrained image-to-video inpainting model using trajectory-adaptive keyframes. VGC learns to map the distorted input to the geometry-consistent target without modifying the source generator, and uses optical flow as an auxiliary cue to help disentangle camera motion from object motion. Experiments on pose-conditioned and prompt-driven benchmarks show that VGC improves geometric consistency across multiple source generators, and scaling to a larger backbone further extends the framework toward more general video refinement.

Rethinking the design space of Vanilla Pixel Diffusion Transformers
Authors: Soumik Mukhopadhyay, Long Mai, Abhinav Shrivastava, Aniruddha Mahapatra
First: 2026-10-16T17:59:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:59:30+00:00
Comments: Project webpage: https://soumik-kanad.github.io/mflow-diffusion/. 33 pages, 23 figures, 15 tables. Submission under review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Pixel-space diffusion models have recently re-emerged as a compelling alternative to latent diffusion, avoiding the lossy latent bottleneck and separately trained autoencoder. JiT shows that a vanilla diffusion transformer can generate directly in pixel space by replacing the commonly used velocity prediction ($\boldsymbol{u}$-prediction) with clean image prediction ($\boldsymbol{x_0}$-prediction). In this work, we revisit this choice and introduce MFlow, an adaptive parameterization that learns to interpolate between $\boldsymbol{x_0}$- and $\boldsymbol{u}$-prediction across principal directions of the data, learned end-to-end with diffusion training. We find that the preferred parameterization varies systematically with the data representation and model capacity -- notably, increasing model capacity shifts preference toward velocity prediction. We evaluate MFlow across ImageNet, UCF-101 video, and large-scale text-to-image generation, demonstrating competitive or improved generative performance across diverse settings. In addition, we find that vanilla pixel diffusion transformers, irrespective of parameterization, retain a persistent high-frequency deficit relative to real images. To address this, we introduce ConvRefiner, a lightweight convolutional module trained post-hoc with an adversarial loss while keeping the diffusion backbone frozen. With only a small amount of additional training, ConvRefiner restores the high-frequency statistics of generated images while preserving FID. StopWatch: Goal Grounding for Object-goal Navigation
Authors: Xinhao Liu, Yunsheng Tian, Yuheng Qiu, Pieter Abbeel, Xi Chen, Guanya Shi, Chen Feng
First: 2026-10-16T17:57:05+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:57:05+00:00
Comments: Project website https://stopwatch-nav.github.io
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Pretrained VLMs bring rich world knowledge and semantic priors to object-goal navigation, and fine-tuning a VLM into a navigation policy has become a common recipe. Despite this progress, success rates remain low, and it is unclear which capability these fine-tuned policies lack. Our diagnosis identifies goal grounding as the dominant failure. These policies explore well, yet often fail to notice that the goal is already in view or to estimate how far away it is. To address this failure, we ground the goal explicitly. Explicit grounding substantially improves success and reaches state-of-the-art results on six benchmarks across three scene collections. We also correct annotation defects in these benchmarks, so that future methods are evaluated more faithfully. Our analysis on the clean benchmarks shows that, with ground-truth grounding, our policy nearly matches the expert that generated its demonstrations, leaving the headroom in perception rather than policy learning. Our work points to goal grounding as a missing piece of object-goal navigation and offers a simple and effective solution to the task.

Abstract
Identity-preserving text-to-video generation (IPT2V) empowers users to produce diverse and imaginative videos with consistent human facial identity. Despite recent progress, existing methods often suffer from significant identity distortion under large facial pose variations or facial occlusions. In this paper, we propose FaithfulFaces, a pose-faithful facial identity preservation learning framework to improve IPT2V in complex dynamic scenes. The key of FaithfulFaces is a pose-shared identity aligner that refines and aligns facial poses across distinct views via a pose-shared dictionary and a pose variation-identity invariance constraint. By mapping single-view inputs into a global facial pose representation with explicit Euler angle embeddings, FaithfulFaces provides a pose-faithful facial prior that guides generative foundations toward robust identity-preserving generation. In particular, we develop a specialized pipeline to curate a high-quality video dataset featuring substantial facial pose diversity. Extensive experiments demonstrate that FaithfulFaces achieves state-of-the-art performance, maintaining superior identity consistency and structural clarity even as pose changes and occlusions occur. Abstract
Purpose: To accelerate chemical exchange saturation transfer magnetic resonance fingerprinting (CEST-MRF) using temporal deep learning reconstruction. Methods: Ten healthy volunteers underwent repeated test-retest golden-angle radial CEST-MRF acquisitions with 30 measurements and 34 spokes per measurement. The Movienet network was adapted to reconstruct accelerated radial CEST-MRF data. Separate models were trained using 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 20, and 34 spokes per measurement. Temporal and coil correlations were exploited by concatenating time and coil dimensions in the input. GRASP-based locally low-rank (GRASP-LLR) reconstruction was used for comparison. Quantitative DRONE fitting generated tissue parameter maps. Movienet-8 was evaluated with leave-one-subject-out analysis. Prospectively acquired 8-spoke datasets were compared with paired 34-spoke acquisitions, and Movienet-8 and Movienet-34 were compared with a 64-spoke acquisition. Results: In the held-out retrospective series, median Pearson correlations between Movienet and GRASP-LLR were 0.795, 0.947, 0.974, 0.977, and 0.981 for 1, 8, 16, 20, and 34 spokes, respectively. Corresponding NRMSE values were 14.7, 8.2, 5.9, 5.3, and 5.1 percent. Across 10 leave-one-subject-out volunteers, median image correlation was 0.965 and NRMSE was 7.5 percent. Reconstruction of a 30-slice series required 1.7 s with Movienet versus approximately 45 min with GRASP-LLR. Conclusion: Movienet enables whole-brain radial CEST-MRF acquisition in 3 min, with image reconstruction in less than 2 s.

Persistent Planar Memory for Video World Models
Authors: Yuze He, Bin Tan, Zelin Gao, Yujun Shen, Yong-Jin Liu, Nan Xue, Yinghao Xu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-16T17:21:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:21:54+00:00
Comments: 20 pages, 9 figures. Accepted by NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Video world models that generate and explore environments at scale need a persistent 3D memory of what they have produced. Plane primitives offer a natural candidate: a compact set of oriented planes captures the dominant structural elements (e.g., walls, floors, facades) of indoor and outdoor scenes, while requiring only 7 parameters per primitive. We present PPMem, a video world model that adopts plane primitives as its persistent 3D memory. PPMem augments a video diffusion transformer with a planar prediction head and a Depth Token Fusion (DTF) module: each autoregressive chunk jointly produces RGB video and planar geometry, which merges into a persistent plane memory that conditions subsequent chunks via rendered depth. The resulting memory is three orders of magnitude more compact than per-pixel point clouds (~100K primitives, 5MB for a full scene), geometrically explicit (metric depth, normals, exportable surfaces), cross-view consistent (jointly predicted by the shared backbone), and adaptive (refined by the merging procedure as generation proceeds). On DL3DV and RealEstate10K, PPMem outperforms prior 3D-grounded world models in video quality while jointly producing metric-scale depth, surface normals, and exportable planar geometry, sustaining coherence across hundreds of frames under large viewpoint changes. Abstract
Central line-associated bloodstream infections (CLABSIs) remain a major source of preventable morbidity and mortality in healthcare settings. Proper execution of the central line dressing change (CLDC) procedure is essential for infection prevention, yet current approaches to competency assessment rely primarily on manual ob- servation by clinical educators, making evaluation time-intensive, difficult to scale, and limited in consistency and granularity. These challenges highlight the need for AI-assisted skill evaluation methods that can support more objective, reproducible, and scalable assessment of clinical performance. However, progress in this area has been constrained by the lack of broad-scale, clinically grounded datasets for developing and evaluating such methods. In this work, we present the collection process of a large-scale video dataset for CLDC skill evaluation, comprising 365 recordings of registered nurses collected during the 2026 annual competency exam at Houston Methodist Hospital. Each video is paired with subtask-level temporal annotations and clinical performance assessments spanning 17 subtasks of the procedure. We describe the data collection and curation pipeline, including pilot testing of record- ing configurations, annotation protocol design, and post-collection processing. We further establish the feasibility of video-based skill assessment through benchmark experiments with spatiotemporal deep learning models. This dataset provides a foundation for future research on automated clinical skill assessment, feedback generation, and AI-assisted training in nursing education.

Localizing and quantifying cerebral arterial stenosis from sparse velocity waveforms using a conditional neural operator
Authors: Tong Zhu, Yi Zhu, Jiawei Liu, Pipi Hu
First: 2026-10-16T17:10:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:10:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cerebral arterial stenosis alters blood flow throughout the vascular network, complicating the estimation of its location and severity from sparse velocity observations. We developed a conditional neural operator that predicts cerebral hemodynamics for candidate stenoses and enables inverse estimation through waveform comparison. Training data were generated using a sequential 0D-1D model that simulates pressure, cross-sectional area, and velocity under varying anatomical, physiological, and stenosis conditions. The operator encodes a single stenosis within vessel tokens and uses global attention to represent its effects across the network. Severity-gated coupling connects these representations to a shared field decoder, while paired healthy simulations constrain baseline predictions. After training, model weights remain fixed, and stenosis location and severity are estimated by minimizing the mismatch between predicted and observed midpoint velocity waveforms. Network-averaged relative validation errors were 0.14% for pressure, 0.59% for area, and 2.79% for velocity. For an eight-vessel observation configuration, localization accuracy across 20 candidate vessels was 99.4% without added noise and 96.0% at the 30% noise setting. Comparisons between configurations with equal numbers of observed vessels showed that measurement placement influenced recovery. Errors and localization ambiguities were greater in selected communicating and distal arteries. These results demonstrate the feasibility of surrogate-based stenosis estimation in simulated cerebral networks and highlight the importance of observation placement. Evaluation was limited to unseen stenosis scenarios within sampled anatomical and physiological conditions. Generalization to new individuals and clinical measurements requires further investigation.

Video Diffusion Avatars: Taming Video Diffusion Models into Controllable 3D Avatars
Authors: Simon Giebenhain, Tobias Kirschstein, Liam Schoneveld, Davide Davoli, Zhe Chen, Matthias Nießner
First: 2026-10-16T17:08:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:08:33+00:00
Comments: Project webiste: https://simongiebenhain.github.io/video-diffusion-avatars/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Video diffusion models (VDMs) trained on internet-scale data encode a rich prior on appearance, motion, and physics that is difficult to replicate with explicit 3D rendering and costly 3D data capture. We introduce Video Diffusion Avatars (VDAs), a method that creates photo-realistic, multi-view consistent avatars from a single image by fine-tuning a VDM for precise control over camera viewpoint, facial expression, and upper-body pose. Departing from 3D morphable models (3DMMs), VDAs use a learned expression space that lifts the expressiveness ceiling of 3DMMs, e.g., for the tongue and mouth interior. However, learned expression codes exhibit heavy viewpoint bias, as our DISTRACT benchmark reveals. We show that this bias can be removed through disentanglement, enabling their use as driving signals for multi-view consistent avatars. Camera and upper-body pose are controlled jointly via proxy-mesh renderings, giving independent control over expression, pose, and viewpoint. A camera-triplet training strategy makes the approach efficient: our models train for 34 hours on a single A100 using only public multi-view data. VDAs outperform state-of-the-art single-image 3D avatars on novel-view reenactment, reducing face LPIPS by 29% and improving perceptual quality (JOD) by 66% on BecomingLit. A static 3DGS reconstruction probe further confirms that our generations are the most multi-view consistent. Built on a video prior, VDAs produce effects beyond the reach of explicit renderers, including physically plausible hair dynamics, shadows, reflections, and refractions.

Abstract
Imaging sonar enables underwater observation in conditions where optical imaging is ineffective due to darkness and turbid water. However, imaging-sonar systems previously used for ecological monitoring have typically been expensive, limiting their accessibility. Consumer forward-looking sonar has the potential to make fish monitoring more accessible, but its noisy imagery makes annotation and the development of automated methods challenging. We introduce the Huntington and Caltech Koi RGB and Sonar dataset (HaCKRS), which pairs Garmin LiveScope imaging-sonar recordings with top-down RGB video of koi carp. The dataset contains more than 105,000 temporally matched frame pairs, spanning approximately 3.3 hours of recordings across four range and orientation configurations. We provide frame-wise metadata, sonar geometry and coordinate information, synchronization data for all four configurations, and planar registration for the three horizontal configurations. The release focuses on paired observations and calibration rather than task-specific fish annotations. We intend HaCKRS to support annotation and the development of fish detection, tracking, and counting methods, and investigations into the potential of consumer sonar for ecological monitoring.

Accelerated Execution of Bayesian Neural Networks using a Single Probabilistic Forward Pass and Code Generation
Authors: Bernhard Klein, Falk Selker, Hendrik Borras, Sophie Steger, Franz Pernkopf, Holger Fröning
First: 2025-11-28T18:35:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:57:06+00:00
Comments: Accepted for publication in ACM Transactions on Architecture and Code Optimization (TACO)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Machine learning models perform well across domains such as diagnostics, weather forecasting, NLP, and autonomous driving, but their limited uncertainty handling restricts use in safety-critical settings. Traditional neural networks often fail to detect out-of-domain (OOD) data and may output confident yet incorrect predictions. Bayesian neural networks (BNNs) address this by providing probabilistic estimates, but incur high computational cost because predictions require sampling weight distributions and multiple forward passes. The Probabilistic Forward Pass (PFP) offers a highly efficient approximation to Stochastic Variational Inference (SVI) by assuming Gaussian-distributed weights and activations, enabling fully analytic uncertainty propagation and replacing sampling with a single deterministic forward pass. We present an end-to-end pipeline for training, compiling, optimizing, and deploying PFP-based BNNs on embedded ARM CPUs. Using the TVM deep learning compiler, we implement a dedicated library of Gaussian-propagating operators for multilayer perceptrons and convolutional neural networks, combined with manual and automated tuning strategies. Ablation studies show that PFP consistently outperforms SVI in computational efficiency, achieving speedups of up to 4200x for small mini-batches. PFP-BNNs match SVI-BNNs on Dirty-MNIST in accuracy, uncertainty estimation, and OOD detection while greatly reducing compute cost. These results highlight the potential of combining Bayesian approximations with code generation to enable efficient BNN deployment on resource-constrained systems.

Abstract
Multi-turn image editing aims to modify an image according to a sequence of instructions, making it better aligned with human-AI collaborative creation than single-turn editing. However, constructing high-quality multi-turn training data remains challenging, as conventional sequential rollout accumulates errors and introduces visual-quality drift across editing turns. To address this challenge, we introduce MTEdit, a multi-turn image editing dataset built through a pipeline centered on packed trajectory canvas (PTC) generation. By jointly generating multiple editing states within a unified visual context, this pipeline reduces visual-quality drift across turns. Covering both step-wise and context-aware editing scenarios, MTEdit comprises 500K multi-turn image editing trajectories, with up to 8 turns and an average of 5.38 turns per trajectory, making it one of the most extensive datasets for multi-turn image editing. We further introduce MTEdit-Bench, a benchmark designed to evaluate both scenarios by assessing models' ability to follow editing instructions, maintain content consistency, resist visual-quality drift, and effectively use historical context across turns. Using MTEdit, we fine-tune Qwen-Image-Edit-Causal to obtain Qwen-Image-MTEdit. On MTEdit-Bench, we evaluate a broad range of closed-source and open-source image editing models, including Qwen-Image-MTEdit. Experimental results show that Qwen-Image-MTEdit achieves the best performance among the evaluated open-source models, demonstrating the effectiveness of MTEdit in improving models' multi-turn image editing capabilities.

Abstract
Retinal fundus images frequently carry more than one disease, and public fundus datasets are heterogeneous and severely long-tailed. Clinical triage suggests a staged design, in which a screening gate, a disease-group router and group-specific classifiers operate in sequence, but it is unclear whether this hierarchy earns its complexity. We present a controlled comparison of three architectures on a unified 39-target label space built from RFMiD 1.0, RFMiD 2.0 and ODIR-5K (12,652 images): (i) a three-stage gated cascade of eight models, (ii) a flat model with 39 independent sigmoid outputs, and (iii) a label-correlation model that replaces the flat linear head with 39 learned label queries and a two-layer transformer decoder. All three share one Swin-V2-B backbone, one preprocessing pipeline and one training protocol, so differences are attributable to the architecture. Staging offers no consistent advantage: tracing every missed disease instance to the stage that lost it shows that the two coarse gates discard 34.2% of true instances while the fine-grained classifiers lose only 6.7%, and the gates' hard zeros depress the cascade's macro-AUC to 0.747. Label-correlation modeling gives the largest single gain, raising the detection rate from 0.621 to 0.682 (0.689 with test-time augmentation). Blending the flat and label-correlation models under four-view test-time augmentation is the best configuration on six of eight metrics (exact match 0.694, micro-F1 0.678, macro-F1 0.456, macro-AUC 0.972). On definition-matched class subsets it matches or exceeds four recent studies on macro- and micro-F1, most clearly on low-support pathologies. We also show that ensembling a gated cascade with continuous-output models is harmful, and that per-source negative sampling is needed to remove a dataset shortcut that otherwise yields a spurious AUC of 1.000.

Abstract
Recent deep facial phenotyping methods can support the recognition of genetic syndromes, but patient images are often scarce and unevenly distributed across disorders. Synthetic facial images offer a way to supplement the training data without collecting additional patient photographs. Their usefulness, however, depends on whether they improve recognition of real patients: generated images may introduce artifacts or repeat features already represented in the training set. It is therefore necessary to examine both how much synthetic augmentation to use and which disorders benefit. We investigate disease-specific synthetic oversampling using images from the Phenotypic Disease Image Database to augment a subset of the GestaltMatcher Database. We compare increasing augmentation levels in an image-based model, GestaltMatcher-Arc, and a multimodal model, GestaltMML, while keeping the real training and patient-disjoint evaluation partitions fixed. Repeated training runs assess overall and disease-specific accuracy, and an exploratory embedding analysis examines changes in class compactness and separation. Augmentation improves mean recognition accuracy in both training setups, although gains remain modest in GestaltMatcher-Arc and continue across the tested levels in GestaltMML. These aggregate improvements are accompanied by uneven disease-level responses, including reduced accuracy for some disorders. In the embedding analysis, disorders with fewer real training images tend to gain more class separation, but geometric changes do not consistently track classification performance. Together, the findings support synthetic oversampling for limited-data facial phenotyping and emphasize the need to assess its amount and benefit for individual disorders within each training setup.

Abstract

Neural Expansion: A Unified Mechanism for How Deep Neural Networks Generalize
Authors: Chashi Mahiul Islam, Samuel Jacob Chacko, Mao Nishino, Canlin Zhang, Xiuwen Liu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-16T16:38:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:38:02+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Includes supplementary material. Code: https://github.com/meetchashi/neural_expansion_neurips_2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Deep neural networks demonstrate remarkable generalization despite being highly over-parameterized, challenging traditional statistical learning theories. While several theoretical paradigms such as margin-based bounds, PAC-Bayesian analyses, and neural tangent kernels provide global explanations, they often lack mechanistic insight into how generalization emerges from training dynamics. We introduce Neural Expansion, a unified geometric framework that characterizes generalization through the expansion of directionally-selective ray coverage in the input space. Using Generalization Intervals (GIs), we quantify direction-specific regions around training samples where the model's predictions remain stable. Our extensive empirical studies across diverse architectures and datasets reveal that training leads to progressive expansion of ray coverage, particularly along directions with low Jacobian singular values. This mechanism helps explain a large fraction of correctly classified test inputs, adversarial examples, and even mislabeled or out-of-distribution samples through the geometry of ray coverage rather than instance-level memorization. By connecting input-space curvature to predictive behavior, our framework provides a mechanistic foundation that complements existing global theories of deep learning.

FedRAPP: Federated Test-Time Prompt Tuning for Vision-Language Models with Reliability-Aware Prompt Personalization
Authors: Syed Md. Ahnaf Hasan, Md Akil Raihan Iftee, Sajib Mistry, Amin Ahsan Ali, AKM Mahbubur Rahman

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Federated prompt learning enables vision-language models like CLIP to collaboratively adapt lightweight soft prompts across distributed clients without sharing raw data. However, existing federated prompt learning methods are designed primarily for supervised settings with labeled client data and assumed relatively stable training distributions. At test time, source-free federated prompt tuning remains largely unexplored. Clients may receive unlabeled target samples with heterogeneous, often non-IID distributions that differ substantially across clients and from the source training distribution, causing locally adapted prompts to drift in different directions and reducing the effectiveness of federated aggregation. In this work, we explore Source-Free Federated Test-Time Prompt Tuning (SF-FTTPT), where distributed clients collaboratively adapt soft prompts on unlabeled test streams without access to source data. Our study reveals that conventional federated aggregation methods struggle under heterogeneous client distributions, as locally adapted prompts become increasingly specialized and difficult to combine effectively. To address this challenge, we propose FedRAPP, a personalized aggregation framework that combines reliability-aware client assessment, server-side exponential moving-average prompt memory, and similarity-guided peer aggregation. FedRAPP selectively incorporates information from reliable and compatible clients while preserving local specialization, enabling more robust adaptation under distribution shift. Abstract
