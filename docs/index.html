Joints, Camera, Action! Co-Generating 3D Motion and Video for Arbitrary Rigs
Authors: Noam Rotstein, Yoad Tewel, Chen Tessler, Haggai Maron, Gal Chechik, Or Litany, Yoni Kasten, Yuval Atzmon
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Video generation models can now depict remarkably realistic motion. Yet it is unclear how to use this motion beyond pixels: to animate a specific 3D asset and replay the animation in its 3D scene. For instance, a video model can show a monkey jumping over a crossbar, but it cannot return that jump as replayable 3D motion placed in the 3D scene. Here we present a model that directly co-generates the video and 3D motion for any supplied rig, along with a camera that registers this motion to the scene. Prior video-motion models either handle only human skeletons, often adding billions of parameters, or triangulate 3D motion from generated 2D pose maps. We instead keep 3D motion in a separate model that supports arbitrary skeletons, and connect it to a pretrained video model through a small set of cross-attention layers. This separation lets the motion model first learn how skeletons move from motion-only data, which is easier to obtain and augment than paired video. Additionally, our model learns to locate each joint in the video frames, which lets us recover the camera and place the 3D motion in the scene. To train across many skeletons, we use a generative video editor to place animated 3D assets in scenes suited to their actions, curating the edits so their known motion remains valid. Together with human and robot video, this yields nearly 200K training pairs. Our evaluations demonstrate that our model animates diverse rigs from text more faithfully than video-first pipelines, and its motion responds to the surrounding scene. It also turns video into rig motion, rig motion into video, and enables keyframe-based control of the 3D motion. Videos and additional results are available on our project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/.

AEON: Unifying Video and 3D World Models
Authors: Weiqi Zhang, Wenyuan Zhang, Yu-Shen Liu, Junsheng Zhou
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:51+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026. Project Page: https://weiqi-zhang.github.io/AEON
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Current world models for explicit world simulation typically follow two distinct pathways: (1) video generative models (e.g., Sora), which simulate realistic appearances but fail to preserve 3D consistency; and (2) 3D generative models (e.g., Marble), which generate geometrically consistent scenes but often yield static, less realistic observations. We present AEON, a unified framework that bridges video-based and 3D-based world models and combines the benefits of both. The key insight of AEON lies in a generative model that operates within the text- or image-conditioned video latent space while producing renderable 4D graphics primitives---specifically, 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS)---as the explicit world representation. We train a 4D reconstruction model to predict both 4DGS and camera poses from monocular videos, serving as our initialized 4D decoder. Furthermore, we design a domain adapter that translates generative video latents into reconstructive 4D latents through a distillation process on the video VAE. AEON naturally supports video-based world modeling by rendering 4DGS at predicted camera trajectories, and constructs a persistent 4D environment with scene dynamics for realistic world simulation. Consequently, AEON empowers a diverse range of world modeling applications and achieves state-of-the-art performance on standard benchmarks. Project Page: https://weiqi-zhang.github.io/AEON

SolarRenderer: Few-Step Generative Reference Rendering
Authors: Guian Fang, Shengju Qian, Dongyue Lu, Junchao Huang, Lutao Jiang, Xin Wang, Yiren Song, Li Jiang, Mike Zheng Shou
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:48+00:00
Comments: Technical Report; 28 pages, 17 figures, 10 tables; Project page at https://enderfga.cn/SolarRenderer Code at https://github.com/Enderfga/SolarRenderer
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Game engines and coding agents can specify the layout, motion, and camera of a scene, yet turning such coarse structure into realistic video requires a renderer that follows the structure and completes the appearance it leaves open. Existing renderers are tied to the representation of one engine, need tens of denoising steps or a costly distillation of their own, and learn from conditions that are not aligned with their target videos. We present SolarRenderer, a structure-conditioned video renderer that adapts a frozen MiniMax-H3 33B backbone with two lightweight LoRAs. Structural videos enter the native reference pathway of the backbone at the positions of the frames they describe, without a type identifier and described only in plain text, so that a single control LoRA follows arbitrary structural representations. Starting from a pretrained four-step LoRA, structure-guided distribution matching renders in four steps, 12.3 times faster than 50-step sampling, while preserving the learned control. A block-causal variant extends the same design to streaming and renders 768p video in real time. To train the model, we build a frame-aligned corpus of 136K clips from game engines, simulators, and YouTube videos. Experiments on an in-distribution test set and a generalization benchmark of 16 structural representations show that SolarRenderer achieves state-of-the-art results across both, and generalizes to 11 unseen representations, including pose skeletons, drawings, and point clouds.

Learning Adaptable Dexterous Skills via Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking
Authors: Yuxuan Kuang, Jiashun Wang, Yixuan Sun, Yufei Ye, Sungjae Park, Umar Iqbal, Katerina Fragkiadaki, Shubham Tulsiani
First: 2026-10-20T17:59:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:59:32+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Dexterous multi-finger hands hold promise to unlock diverse skills like functional grasps, in-hand reorientation, and dynamic handover that the human-designed world assumes, yet existing learning recipes force a trade-off. Goal-reaching reinforcement learning generalizes to arbitrary object poses but, lacking any human prior, converges to unnatural and unreliable finger strategies; motion tracking reproduces human-like behavior from a single clip but cannot deviate from it. We present AdaDex, a goal-conditioned RL framework that turns a single hand-object trajectory of a few seconds---from mocap data or HOI reconstruction from in-the-wild RGB(D) video---into an adaptable dexterous skill. At the core of AdaDex is Hybrid Dexterous Mimicking: a shared policy is trained on two populations of massively parallel environments: motion tracking environments trained with dense motion tracking objective, which instill the human prior from human motion; and generalization environments with noised initial states and goals and a sparse object-success reward, which force the skill to work far off the reference. From seconds of human motion per skill, the resulting policies can learn diverse adaptable dexterous skills, such as functional grasping, object reposing, in-hand reorientation with finger gaiting, and dynamic behaviors like throw-and-catch. Our method outperforms baselines in terms of generalization and functionality, and we demonstrate transfer across simulators and to the real world. Project page: https://yuxuank.com/AdaDex/.

UniWALM: Unified World Action Language Model for Embodied Robot Control
Authors: Rahul Sajnani, Diego Romeres, Mingyuan Zhang, Kai Gao, Carl Qi, Baichuan Huang, Srinath Sridhar, Dylan Randle, Lillian J. Ratliff, Jose Barreiros, Manikantan Nambi, Jeroen Vanbaar

Comments: Preprint: 25 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Motivated by the human ability to integrate experience across language, perception, and action, we introduce UniWALM, a unified world-action-language model that learns video, action, and language in a single shared representation, where each modality is both a conditioning signal and a supervision target. We ask: can jointly generating language enrich this shared representation, improving manipulation and supporting memory-dependent behavior? Unlike prior approaches that attach a reasoning or vision-language model to a video-action backbone and run them as distinct stages, UniWALM unifies all three modalities inside a single mixture-of-transformers backbone whose per-modality diffusion heads co-denoise synchronously, decoding subtask and textual memory in the same pass as action with no separate planning stage; the generated language is re-consumed on the next step, closing a language-as-memory loop without an external planner or memory bank. To keep the action stream reliable under this richer objective, we further introduce Flow Matching with Correction (FMC), a training objective that teaches recovery from perturbed action trajectories at no inference-time cost. UniWALM consistently improves over same-backbone baselines (e.g., FastWAM) on compositional pick-and-place (MolmoSpaces) and across the memory-dependent subset of RMBench, where it reaches 34.5% without any pretrained language or reasoning model and exceeds Mem-0's keyframe-equipped setup (28.5%) using only a single anchor frame. On a real-world Find-the-Item task, generating textual memory lifts success from 11.1% to 61.1%, while state-of-the-art VLAs stay at or below chance (<=33.3%).

Abstract
Understanding human needs and selecting responses require distinct judgments that aggregate accuracy can obscure. We introduce PsycheMME, a video benchmark informed by psychology, with eight capabilities spanning grounding, states, needs, interaction decisions, memory, and risk. Drawing on Social Information Processing and the Perception-Action Cycle, this organization separates human understanding from response selection. We construct 1,000 questions from 630 episodes through independent answering with proposed references withheld and expert review. Our Reasoning-Verified protocol weights answer credit by the support and logical consistency of explicit rationales against annotated cues. Across 17 models, Timing has the lowest Verified scores and credit retention, with weaker support judgments for correct Timing answers. Models with similar Need scores still differ in answer completeness on open questions. Input controls and SFT/GRPO examine context dependence and adaptation. Evaluating answers alongside rationales locates where models fail across understanding, response selection, and justification.

MindView: Benchmarking Generative Multi-View Spatial Intelligence
Authors: Yanbo Wang, Siqi Luo, Siyu Zhang, Chenming Zhu, Wenzhao Zheng, Qian Yu, Juncheng Yan, Bin Fu, Tai Wang, Yu Qiao, Yihao Liu, Jiwen Lu
First: 2026-10-20T17:58:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:58:25+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Spatial intelligence has recently been assessed through generative tasks such as spatially grounded image editing, yet language-guided generation of spatially consistent views from partial multi-view observations remains underexplored. We introduce MindView, a benchmark for generative multi-view spatial intelligence with 1,500 samples from 513 scenes across three indoor datasets. Its seven tasks form two families: geometry-dominant tasks specify changes in camera position and orientation, while semantics-dominant tasks require inferring target viewpoints by grounding referred objects and interpreting spatial relations and reference-frame cues. Evaluation of eleven generators reveals substantial room for improvement on MindView. A paired context analysis further shows that all eleven generators obtain lower overall scores under the expanded context setting. These findings motivate us to investigate whether task-specific supervision can improve target-view generation and whether models can use additional multi-view evidence effectively. We therefore fine-tune BAGEL on the MindView training dataset, improving its overall score by 24.3 points and yielding gains across all seven MindView tasks. Beyond these generation gains, the fine-tuned model also yields higher scores on three spatial-understanding benchmarks without training on their annotations, suggesting that learning to generate spatially grounded views may benefit spatial understanding. To improve evidence utilization under expanded context, we propose MindCue, a training-free spatial evidence selector guided by a target-view hypothesis. MindCue reverses the drop in overall score under expanded context and outperforms the standard-context baseline. Overall, MindView provides a concrete testbed for generative multi-view spatial intelligence.

TAPDreamer: Transferable Adversarial Patches for World Action Models
Authors: Xuanyu Lu, Fengqing Jiang, Kaiyuan Zheng, Yichen Feng, Yaorui Ding, Yuetai Li, Zhen Xiang, Bhaskar Ramasubramanian, Basel Alomair, Luyao Niu, Radha Poovendran
First: 2026-10-05T17:55:21+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:55:12+00:00
Comments: Project Page: https://tapdreamer.github.io
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
World models learn to predict how their environment will evolve, making them an important foundation for general-purpose robotic control. Yet world action models depend on camera inputs whose manipulation can corrupt the visual representations used across tasks and action policies. Existing attacks on these models optimize against the victim's actions or predicted futures and therefore require access to target-model outputs. In this paper, we propose an attack, TAPDreamer, against world action models that instead uses a public encoder alone to construct a fixed local perturbation that transfers across tasks and action architectures. TAPDreamer requires no target-policy queries. Our key insight is that interactions between patch-induced changes in attention weights and value vectors broadcast a nearly identical representation shift far beyond the patch footprint, and this shift remains stable across task observations. Guided by this insight, TAPDreamer uses six frames from one source task to maximize the global L1 distance between clean and patched encoder representations. In closed-loop evaluation, one frozen patch per benchmark, covering about 6.5% of the input, reduces FastWAM's success rate from 97.7% to 0.0% across 40 LIBERO tasks and from 90.86% to 0.0% across 50 RoboTwin tasks; matched random patches retain 81.5% and 79.2% success. The same patches reduce success to 1.45% and 1.00% on two DreamWAM configurations and to 10.60% on Motus. These results show that protecting downstream action generation alone is insufficient: defenses for world action models must also secure shared visual encoders against persistent local perturbations.

KoalaGS: Complexity-Aware Continuous Level of Detail for Gaussian Splatting
Authors: Radu Beche, Raoul de Charette
Venue: NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight
First: 2026-10-20T17:55:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:55:06+00:00
Comments: NeurIPS 2026 (Spotlight). Project page https://rdbch.github.io/koalags
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Deploying a trained 3D Gaussian Splatting scene under shrinking compute budgets requires one primitive set to remain effective when rendered at smaller prefixes. In this continuous level of detail (LoD) setting, the challenge is to decide which Gaussians should retain capacity as the budget decreases. A pilot study on 3DGS-MCMC reconstructions shows that image complexity, computed from the input views before optimization, predicts where reconstruction error concentrates in image and 3D space. It also reveals that simple regions contain too many Gaussians and degrade as much as complex ones at lower budgets, despite being inherently cheaper to represent. Subsequently, in KoalaGS, during training we use this complexity prior to bias relocation toward poorly reconstructed areas while ensuring smooth degradation across LoDs. A primitive importance score then enables inference at low-capacity, while preserving the scene structure. Tested across three datasets, KoalaGS maintains competitive full-capacity quality while significantly improving quality across the budget spectrum, yielding smoother transitions across LoDs, and enabling faster progressive streaming.

Neural Implicit Energy Optimization for LiDAR Mapping
Authors: Hrishikesh Viswanath, Md Ashiqur Rahman, Chi Lin, Damon Conover, Aniket Bera
First: 2026-10-20T17:47:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:47:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Neural implicit representations provide memory-efficient signed distance field (SDF) parameterizations for large-scale LiDAR scenes, but the geometry recovered from sparse and often noisy inputs carries jitter and artifacts. Classical geometric optimization techniques resolve such artifacts with surface energy functionals, whose minimizers correspond to smooth surfaces. Motivated by this principle, we formulate neural LiDAR mapping as the optimization of energy functionals over the parameter space of neural implicit representations. Specifically, we regularize the signed distance field with an energy functional defined on the shape operator of its level set, which represents the curvature of the surface, thus encouraging smoothness along the surface. This adapts classical geometric priors to the scale of million-point LiDAR maps. Experiments on large-scale datasets show reconstructions with improved smoothness at preserved geometric fidelity, and no additional inference-time cost. The code is publicly available at \href{https://github.com/HrishikeshVish/nieo}{https://github.com/HrishikeshVish/nieo}

Authors: Byungwoo Jeon, Sunghwan Hong, Dohyeon Kim, Jonghoon Lee, Jungwoo Park, Inhee Lee, Doosan Baek, Jinil Kim, Taehyun Lim, Hanbyul Joo, Marc Pollefeys, Saining Xie, Jinwoo Shin

Abstract
Reliable multi-view forecasting requires videos from different cameras to describe the same evolving 3D scene. To this end, we introduce Video-Point Model (VPM), a world model that conditions on multi-view video histories and a language instruction to jointly forecast future videos from arbitrary viewpoints and the 3D trajectories of persistent scene points. We extend a pretrained world model with camera-conditioned multi-view processing that integrates complementary observations across cameras, and add a trajectory head that predicts how physical points move through time. Each trajectory tracks a single point across all views, and supervising these trajectories alongside video generation therefore anchors appearance changes in every camera to a shared description of 3D motion. To support training and evaluation, we build VPM-Data, a simulation corpus pairing synchronized multi-view videos with persistent point trajectories, and VP-WorldBench, a forecasting benchmark spanning simulated and real environments. We further extend VPM to robot control by adding robot actions as a token modality in the generator. Action tokens share the point-supervised backbone and are denoised jointly with future videos, thereby grounding the policy in the same 3D-aware representation. On VP-WorldBench and existing 4D generation benchmarks, point supervision improves both video fidelity and 3D motion accuracy over a video-only counterpart, and in simulated and real-robot experiments, it also raises the manipulation success of the action-enabled model.

Abstract
Camera-trap imagery presents substantial variation in animal appearance, illumination, background clutter, and visual degradation. Masked autoencoders (MAEs) offer a label-free framework for modeling such data through reconstruction of randomly hidden image patches. We present WildMAE, a decoder-side extension of MAE that combines local structure-aware refinement with masked-to-visible cross-attention. A zero-gated convolutional residual path introduces a two-dimensional neighborhood prior, while gated cross-attention allows masked positions to retrieve contextual information from visible-position features without replacing the original Transformer decoder. The associated training recipe progressively introduces normalized-pixel, Sobel-edge, and perceptual objectives. In the original Snapshot Serengeti study, covering 4,744 test images at 75% masking, the reported reconstruction scores were 27.90 versus 24.33 dB PSNR, 0.840 versus 0.780 SSIM, and 0.160 versus 0.320 LPIPS for WildMAE and vanilla MAE, respectively. Additional historical records document favorable average differences under four synthetic degradation conditions and describe component variants. We present the method, experimental observations, and their scope together: the retained metrics are historical protocol-dependent results, rather than a newly reproduced, harmonized comparison or a validation of restoration from genuinely unavailable pixels. WildMAE provides a practical architectural case study for combining global token mixing with spatial and targeted contextual refinement in wildlife masked-image modeling.

Abstract

Abstract
Audio-visual segmentation (AVS) plays a critical role in multimodal machine learning by effectively integrating audio and visual cues to precisely segment objects or regions within visual scenes. Recent AVS methods have demonstrated significant improvements. However, they overlook the inherent frequency-domain contradictions between audio and visual modalities--the substantial noise often present in audio high-frequency signals vs. the structurally rich details in visual high-frequency signals. Ignoring these differences can result in suboptimal performance. In this paper, we rethink the AVS task from a deeper perspective by reformulating AVS task as a frequency-domain decomposition and recomposition problem. To this end, we introduce a novel Frequency-Aware Audio-Visual Segmentation (FAVS) framework consisting of two key modules: Frequency-Domain Enhanced Decomposer (FDED) module and Synergistic Cross-Modal Consistency (SCMC) module. FDED module employs a residual-based iterative frequency decomposition to discriminate modality-specific semantics and structural features, and SCMC module leverages a mixture-of-experts architecture to reinforce semantic consistency and modality-specific feature preservation through dynamic expert routing. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our FAVS framework achieves state-of-the-art performance on three benchmark datasets, and abundant qualitative visualizations further verify the effectiveness of the proposed FDED and SCMC modules. Abstract
ScreenCalib enables light detection and ranging (LiDAR)--camera extrinsic calibration using an ordinary programmable display, providing known visual markers and controlled geometry while reducing dedicated target preparation. The camera observes displayed AprilTags, whereas the LiDAR measures the physical screen surface and boundary, requiring indirect cross-modal association. Display geometry introduces association ambiguities, while indoor clutter complicates extraction. ScreenCalib addresses these challenges through tag-guided display extraction, geometric hypothesis generation and refinement, and surrounding scene edges for ambiguity resolution. The single-pose mode requires only one static placement. The joint multi-pose mode combines independent static observations through cross-pose consensus and jointly refines the shared extrinsics and tag scale, reducing sensitivity to tag-size measurement errors without requiring continuous motion or inter-pose trajectory estimation. Experiments in simulation and on three different sensor platforms cover mechanical and nonrepetitive LiDARs in clean and cluttered environments. Benchmark results show that ScreenCalib outperforms competitive calibration-board-based methods in translation accuracy. Joint multi-pose refinement further reduces mean rotation and translation errors across the three platforms and improves rendered-map alignment. Evaluation across readily available displays, including cell phones, tablets, laptops, computer monitors, and televisions, further examines ScreenCalib's versatility as a practical alternative to dedicated calibration targets. The source code is available at https://github.com/ZuhaoZou2026/ScreenCalib.

Abstract
Text-to-image personalization aims to equip pretrained diffusion models with the ability to learn user-specific concepts from only a few reference images. Existing methods, however, typically train each concept independently or assume that all concepts are available simultaneously. In practical applications, concepts often arrive sequentially, requiring models to continually learn new subjects, objects, or styles while minimizing interference with previously acquired concepts. We address this continual concept personalization setting by extending residual-space adaptation to the stochastic adaptation process of pretrained diffusion models. Our work achieves the highest concept fidelity among competing continual personalization methods while maintaining near-zero forgetting. Restricting adaptation to the identified layers by our method preserves nearly the same performance while reducing the number of trainable parameters by up to 72\%, demonstrating that residual-space dynamics can identify the layers that contribute most to continual concept personalization and directly guide more parameter-efficient adaptation.

Abstract
Stochastic gradient descent (SGD) is the cornerstone of modern deep learning, yet its theoretical convergence properties remain poorly understood in non-convex settings. To address this gap, we study SGD dynamics in deep linear neural networks - simplified models that retain key non-convex characteristics while remaining analytically tractable. Building on recent results for gradient descent, we extend convergence guarantees to SGD under regularized squared loss. We establish almost sure boundedness of SGD iterates. Using the framework of asymptotic pseudotrajectories from stochastic approximation theory, our analysis establishes then, for both deterministic and adaptive step-size choices, convergence of SGD to the set of critical points under mild conditions (without relying on Lipschitz properties or whitened data). To our knowledge, this is the first work providing almost sure convergence guarantees for SGD in deep linear networks, bridging the gap between theoretical optimization and practical stochastic training.

Abstract
Do visual tasks have a relationship, or are they unrelated? For instance, could having surface normals simplify estimating the depth of an image? Intuition answers these questions positively, implying existence of a structure among visual tasks. Knowing this structure has notable values; it is the concept underlying transfer learning and provides a principled way for identifying redundancies across tasks, e.g., to seamlessly reuse supervision among related tasks or solve many tasks in one system without piling up the complexity. We proposes a fully computational approach for modeling the structure of space of visual tasks. This is done via finding (first and higher-order) transfer learning dependencies across a dictionary of twenty six 2D, 2.5D, 3D, and semantic tasks in a latent space. Abstract
Despite the success of video world models, extending them to multiple agents is difficult because it requires generating several agent-specific video streams that are consistent in time and between agents. Abstract
Autoregressive language models are trained by next-token cross-entropy, yet several recent systems also iterate a hidden state without emitting a token: continuous thoughts, pause steps, and recurrent depth. This note separates three risks on one latent process. The token risk $R_{\mathrm{tok}}$ scores a readout. The teacher-forced latent risk $R_1$ scores a one-step map that sees the true hidden state. The open-loop risk $R_H$ scores the same map after it has been iterated on its own outputs. If the Bayes next-token posterior is a function of a $k$-dimensional statistic with full-rank covariance, a width-$k$ latent, a linear least-squares transition, and a refit unembedding match the Bayes token predictor.
